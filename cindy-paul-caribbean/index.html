Port guide

Where you land
The ship ties up at the Bridgetown Cruise Terminal in the Deep Water Harbour, so you walk straight off. No tender boat needed.
Getting to town
Downtown Bridgetown is about a mile east: a 15–30 minute walk along the harbour, or about 5 minutes by taxi.
Taxis
Government-set fares (Road Traffic Regulations, in force since Jan 7, 2023), quoted in Barbados dollars (BBD $2 = US$1). Taxis have no meters, so agree the price before you get in. Fares are posted at the terminal.
  • Harbour to downtown Bridgetown: BBD $19 (about US$9.50)
  • Bridgetown to St. Lawrence Gap: BBD $40 (about US$20); to Holetown: BBD $62 (about US$31)
  • Bridgetown to Grantley Adams Airport: BBD $77 (about US$38.50)
Walking
The harbour-to-town walk is flat but hot with little shade, and it runs past a busy road. Downtown has sidewalks but they are narrow and crowded in places.
Accessibility
Docked ship, so no tender. Taxis wait right outside the terminal, which makes this the easiest port of the trip for limited mobility.

Port guide

Where you land
Tenders land at the Pointe Séraphine cruise terminal (duty-free mall, north side of the harbour) or at La Place Carenage right in downtown Castries; the ship will announce which.
Getting to town
From La Place Carenage you are in town, a few minutes' walk to Derek Walcott Square and the market; from Pointe Séraphine it is about a 15–20 minute walk around the harbour or a short water-taxi or car ride.
Taxis
Fares are set by government in a schedule (EC$ and US$), but taxis are unmetered, so always agree the price, and which currency, before you set off. Prices are usually per car.
  • Castries to Rodney Bay / Reduit Beach: about US$20–30 per car (Frommer's says $20–25; GoByTaxi lists $30)
  • Castries to Morne Fortune (Pink Plantation House): short ride, likely under US$20; confirm with the driver
Walking
Downtown Castries is flat and compact, but sidewalks are narrow and traffic is heavy. Anything beyond town (Morne Fortune, Rodney Bay, Marigot) is up steep, winding roads, so take a taxi. It's a Sunday, so many shops and the market will be quiet or closed.
Accessibility
Tender port: you step from the ship onto a small boat and up onto a pier, which can be hard if the sea is choppy. Guests who cannot manage a few steps should check with Seabourn before going ashore.

Port guide

Where you land
Ships land guests at the Little Bay jetty / ferry terminal on the island's northwest coast; most ships anchor and tender in, so expect a tender unless Seabourn says otherwise.
Getting to town
Little Bay is the island's small hub, and Brades (the main village and government centre) is right next door; the volcano sights (Montserrat Volcano Observatory, views of buried Plymouth) need a taxi or tour.
Taxis
Official maximum fares exist and are posted at the port, but the only published list is very old, so most visitors hire a driver-guide for an island tour at an agreed price.
  • Taxi island tour (volcano views, Observatory, Plymouth lookouts): about US$120 for 1–4 people, roughly 3–3.5 hours (visitor report)
  • Seabourn's own 'Buried City & Observatory' tour: from about US$120 per person, about 2.5 hours
Walking
Montserrat is very hilly and there are few sidewalks. You can stroll the Little Bay beach and port area, but plan on a taxi for everything else.
Accessibility
Likely a tender landing at a small jetty that can be exposed to swell, then hilly roads, so this port is hard for limited mobility; check with Seabourn.

Port guide

Where you land
Ships dock at a pier right at the edge of downtown St. John's (Heritage Quay or the neighbouring Redcliffe Quay / Nevis Street pier), so you walk off into the shopping area.
Getting to town
You are in town as soon as you step off; St. John's Cathedral is a few blocks uphill, and the beaches are 10–40 minutes away by taxi.
Taxis
Fares are set by the Antigua & Barbuda Transport Board and posted at the taxi dispatch kiosk at Heritage Quay. Prices are usually per car in US dollars, but confirm per-car vs per-person and agree before you go.
  • St. John's to Dickenson Bay: about US$13–15 per car
  • St. John's to English Harbour / Nelson's Dockyard: about US$25–45 per car (some drivers will wait while you look around for about US$50)
Walking
Downtown is compact and mostly flat near the quays, with a gentle climb to the cathedral. Sidewalks are narrow or missing in spots, traffic drives on the left, and it gets hot by midday.
Accessibility
Docked ship with level quay access to the duty-free area and taxi rank, so it's fairly easy. Old-town streets have uneven curbs.

Getting to town
Everything at Soper's Hole is steps from the tender landing. Road Town, the capital, and the north-shore beaches (Smuggler's Cove, Long Bay West, Cane Garden Bay) need a taxi.
Taxis
BVI fares are set by law (Road Traffic Passenger Fares Regulations, amended Dec 2022). Island tours are priced per group, and cruise-dock fares are per person, dropping as the group grows. The official table lists fares from the Road Town cruise dock, not from West End, so agree West End fares before you ride.
  • Island tour, official 2022 rate: $70 total for 1-2 people for 1 hour, $110 for 2 hours, $165 for 3 hours. Groups of 3 pay $85, $130 or $190.
  • For comparison, the official fares from the Road Town cruise dock to Cane Garden Bay are $24 for 1 person, $16 each for 2, and $12 each for 3 or more. To Smuggler's Cove they are $32, $18 each, or $15 each (2022). From West End, Smuggler's Cove and Long Bay are much closer, so expect less.
  • Unofficial: one Cruise Critic poster reported about $15 per person from Cane Garden Bay to West End.
Government-set. Cruise-dock fares are per person, and island tours are a total fare per group.
Walking
Soper's Hole is tiny and flat, so you can stroll the whole marina in 15-20 minutes. Beyond it the roads are steep, winding and have no sidewalks, so don't try to walk to the beaches.
Accessibility
The marina boardwalk is mostly level, but tender transfers depend on sea conditions. Tortola's steep roads make taxis or a tour the only practical way to explore with limited mobility.

Port guide

Seabourn lists Trois-Îlets as a tender port. The ship anchors in the Bay of Fort-de-France, and tenders most likely land at the Pointe du Bout marina, the resort, restaurant and marina hub of Les Trois-Îlets. The exact pier isn't confirmed, so check the ship's daily program.
Getting to town
From Pointe du Bout marina, Anse Mitan beach and Pointe du Bout beach are a 5-10 minute walk. The village (bourg) of Trois-Îlets and La Pagerie (Empress Josephine's childhood home) are a short taxi ride away, not walking distance. Fort-de-France is about 15-20 minutes across the bay by ferry.
Taxis
Prices are in euros (€), and taxis in Martinique are pricey. Fares go up about 40% at night, on Sundays and on holidays. Agree the price before you get in, and expect drivers to speak mostly French.
  • Ferry (vedette/navette) from Pointe du Bout marina to Fort-de-France (Pointe Simon pier): about 15-20 minutes, from €5 one-way or €7 round trip per adult. Run by Blue Lines since July 2024, daily roughly 5:50am-10pm. This is much cheaper than a taxi into the capital.
  • Taxi from Fort-de-France to any Pointe du Bout hotel: about €65 (older guide).
  • Taxi from the airport to Trois-Îlets/Anse Mitan: about €61 on average (2025 quote aggregator, range €30-128+).
Metered or quoted per car, not per person. Martinique taxis are regulated, but no public cruise rate card was found.
Walking
The Pointe du Bout area is compact and pleasant on foot, with the marina, shops, a small Creole village of boutiques and two beaches. Getting to the Trois-Îlets bourg, La Pagerie or the Savane des Esclaves needs a taxi or a tour.
Accessibility
The marina quays and nearby lanes are fairly level, but tender boarding can be tricky in swell. For limited mobility, a ship tour or pre-booked driver is easier than finding taxis.

Getting to town

Taxis

Set rates. Sources disagree on per person vs per car, so ask the dispatcher before you ride.
Walking

Accessibility
